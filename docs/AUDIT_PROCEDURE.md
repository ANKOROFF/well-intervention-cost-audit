# Audit procedure

The audit tests whether the cost model behind an intervention-selection program can be trusted. It uses only what a registry of past operations already holds: dated operations, equipment events, recorded material volumes and the pump designation strings, plus a response model trained on earlier years and a table of unit prices. It needs no new data collection and no new cost study. An operator can repeat it in six steps (Fig. 1 of the paper).

1. **Read the cost-driver quantities from the records.** For every candidate operation take durations, idle time, equipment events, recorded material volumes and the pump designation before and after the operation. Mark each quantity as observed (read from a structured table), parsed (read from a text field by a parser) or inferred (filled by a type-level rule or a forecast). Validate the parser on a labeled sample and on the synthetic test set in `tests/`. Modules: `cost_physical.py`, `crew_model.py`, `provenance.py`, `parser_validation.py`.
2. **Build at least two cost representations of every candidate.** For example an itemized one (quantities times unit prices) and a day-rate one (duration times a daily rate), both calibrated to the same total CAPEX. The comparison then concerns the structure of the cost model and not its level. Modules: `cost_physical.py`, `capex_exante.py`, `ep06_ablation.py`.
3. **Predict the effect.** Fit a response model on earlier years only (walk-forward) to forecast the incremental oil of each candidate, and compute NPV under each cost representation with the same prices and discount rate. Modules: `stats.py`, `npv.py`, `dur_model.py`.
4. **Run the same selection under each representation.** Use the same greedy selection under the budget and crew-day capacity of each year, once per representation and under many draws of the price vector. Compute the Jaccard overlap of the selected sets and the share of the gap closed (GC) against the perfect-information benchmark, with bootstrap intervals over years. Modules: `stats.py`, `oracle.py`, `ep30_crossacct.py`, `price_montecarlo.py`, `ep30_pselect.py`.
5. **List the operations that depend on the choice.** These are the operations selected under one representation and not the other, and the draw-sensitive operations, selected in more than 5% and fewer than 95% of the price draws. The sign-uncertain classes over the box of admissible prices are a diagnostic and do not enter the selection. Modules: `ep30_pselect.py`, `ep30_ident.py`, `figures/fig_flips.py`.
6. **Send these operations, and only these, to an engineer for a cost check.** In the paper, over the nine selection years, the two selections differ in 229 of 2,993 operations and 431 operations are draw-sensitive. 129 are both, so the list holds 531 operations, or 17.7%, instead of the whole candidate list. In 2024 it holds 85 of 238.

The list shows which operations a cost check should cover first. It does not show that the check changes realized value, and it does not show which of the two representations the engineer should believe. The paper did not time the work and states nothing about person-hours.
